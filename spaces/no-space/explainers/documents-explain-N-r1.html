Large-scale document classification

An insurer receives between two and eight million documents a day (scanned forms, e-mails, PDFs) and classifies each one into one of 60 claim types with an LLM-based classifier. Volume varies strongly over the year: storm season brings four times the usual load. Three teams use the results: claims handling, fraud investigation and analytics. The documents contain personal data.

The solution

S · Document classification as built

An EU hosted, event-driven LLM pipeline classifies documents, routes uncertain results to staff, and publishes results for independent team consumption. Prompt changes are based on corrections and quality is monitored per claim type.

ADR1 · Scale document inference while keeping data in the EU R1Q1Q3C1C2

Context. The system must classify every input with confidence, handle the seasonal peak, control average inference cost, and keep personal data within the EU while using the approved EU-region LLM.

Decision. EU event intake, elastic Kubernetes workers, and batched approved EU inference.

EU event intake, elastic Kubernetes workers, and batched approved EU inference chosen

Intake → EU object storage with Kafka upload eventsPreprocessing → OCR, content-hash deduplication, and adjacent extracted textClassifier runtime → Kubernetes workers autoscaled from 4 to 120 by Kafka backlogLLM → approved provider in its EU regionPrompt → 60 claim definitions and ten labelled examplesInference batching → batches of 20 small documents

“Incoming documents land in an object storage bucket in the EU region, and each upload puts a message on a Kafka topic.”
“A preprocessing service reads the topic, extracts the text (with OCR for scanned forms), removes duplicates by content hash and writes the text next to the document.”
“The number of classifier workers scales with the length of the Kafka topic's backlog, from 4 to 120.”
“A classifier service, running on Kubernetes, sends the text to the approved provider's LLM in its EU region.”
“The prompt lists the 60 claim types with a short definition and ten labelled examples, and asks for a claim type and a confidence between 0 and 1.”
“Small documents are sent in batches of 20 to reduce cost.”
  • Processes scans and extracts usable text.
  • Backlog-based scaling responds to changing volume.
  • Batching is intended to reduce cost.
  • EU storage and approved EU inference meet the location and provider constraints.
  • Batch filling may add latency.
  • The worker range and batch size are not backed by throughput or cost measurements.

The approved EU provider is forced by C2 and EU handling by C1. Event buffering, Kubernetes, the scaling range, prompt format, and batch size are implementation choices; throughput and cost targets remain unverified.

ADR2 · Publish results for independent team consumption R2Q4

Context. Claims handling, fraud investigation, and analytics all need results, and each team must be able to change its processing independently.

Decision. Shared Kafka results topic with team consumer groups.

Shared Kafka results topic with team consumer groups chosen

Result provenance → prompt version and model name stored with each resultResult delivery → second Kafka topic with one consumer group per teamAnalytics delivery → hourly warehouse load

“Every result is stored with the prompt version and the model name that produced it.”
“Results go to a second Kafka topic. Each consuming team has its own consumer group and reads at its own pace;”
“analytics also loads the topic into the data warehouse every hour.”
  • Each team can consume at its own pace.
  • Consumer changes are isolated from the classifier and other teams.
  • Analytics receives a regular warehouse feed.
  • Kafka topic and consumer operations add infrastructure and maintenance.

Independent consumption is required by Q4; Kafka consumer groups and the hourly warehouse schedule are free choices. Stored provenance supports later diagnosis.

ADR3 · Route uncertain classifications into a correction loop R1R3

Context. The classifier must return a claim type and confidence, send uncertain results to staff, and use corrections to improve future classifications.

Decision. Confidence threshold, staff labelling, monthly prompt examples, and pre-release check.

Confidence threshold, staff labelling, monthly prompt examples, and pre-release check chosen

Human review → confidence below 0.7 enters labelling queuePrompt learning → monthly replacement of examples with corrected documentsPrompt validation → check against 2,000 reviewed documents before switch

“Documents with a confidence below 0.7 go to a review queue in a labelling tool, where claims staff correct the claim type.”
“Every month the team replaces the prompt's examples with recent corrected documents,”
“and checks the new prompt against 2,000 reviewed documents before switching to it.”
  • Staff corrections feed future prompt examples.
  • A fixed review threshold gives an explicit routing rule.
  • The prompt is checked before a new version is used.
  • The threshold and monthly cadence may not match changing uncertainty or drift.
  • Review and validation create delay and staff effort.

Reviewing uncertain items and using corrections are required by R3. The 0.7 cutoff, monthly cadence, replacement strategy, and sample size are freely selected parameters.

ADR4 · Watch quality by claim type Q2

Context. Operators need to notice when classification quality degrades for a particular claim type within one day.

Decision. Per-type dashboard, review-share alert, and weekly sample.

Per-type dashboard, review-share alert, and weekly sample chosen

Quality monitoring → per-claim review share and mean confidence dashboard with doubling alertQuality audit → weekly random sample of 300 classifications

“A dashboard shows, per claim type, the share of documents sent to review and the mean confidence; an alert fires when the share sent to review for a claim type doubles from one week to the next.”
“Each week, analysts review a random sample of 300 classified documents.”
  • Per-claim measures can reveal localized changes.
  • The weekly human sample checks a signal beyond model confidence.
  • Review share and mean confidence are indirect quality measures.
  • Weekly comparison and sampling do not establish one-day detection.

Per-type visibility supports Q2, but the solution does not demonstrate that the alert fires within one day. The selected indicators, doubling rule, sample size, and cadence are not forced by the requirement.
