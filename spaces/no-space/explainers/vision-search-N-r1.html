On-device visual inspection

A manufacturer inspects parts for defects with cameras along its production lines. There are 40 lines in three plants, each with two to four cameras attached to a small edge computer next to the line. The plants' network is unreliable: some lines are cut off for days at a time. Quality inspectors label images of defects, about 5,000 new labels a week, and the model is retrained every week from them and pushed to the devices. The team is two ML engineers and one person who spends half their time on operations. The infrastructure budget is tight.

Every judgement here is the agent's; the page lays out its rationale file.

ADR1 · Where should inspection inference run? R1Q1Q2C2

Context. Every part must be inspected within 200 ms while production continues through week-long plant network outages. The ARM edge computers cannot be replaced this year, so inference must use the installed devices and be validated against their accelerator and model capacity.

Decision. Edge-only inference (A1, B1, C1, D1).

Edge-only inference chosen in A1, B1, C1, D1

Inference → Edge-only on-device inference

  • Inspection remains available without cloud connectivity.
  • The network is not on the per-image classification path.
  • The model must fit and meet the latency target on the existing ARM accelerator.

All four alternatives preserve line operation during outages and avoid replacing the constrained edge computers.

ADR2 · How should selected images feed weekly retraining? R2R3Q2

Context. Inspectors label about 5,000 new defect images weekly, and retraining must use new labels. Images need to survive network outages, while storing and uploading all camera traffic would increase bandwidth and storage needs. Capture should focus on uncertain or incorrect predictions.

Decision. Managed weekly training with selective capture (A1, B1, C1, D1); Device queues with resumable upload (A1, C1); Per-camera queues relayed through plant gateway (B1); Persistent gateway spool (D1).

Managed weekly training with selective capture chosen in A1, B1, C1, D1

Training → Managed weekly cloud retrainingCapture policy → Uncertainty and error-triggered image capture

  • Automates a weekly retraining cadence.
  • Targets inspector effort and image transfer at informative or incorrect cases.
  • Training reflects uploaded samples and may lag live production changes.

This covers weekly model updates and the requested feedback loop while limiting unnecessary image collection.

Device queues with resumable upload chosen in A1, C1

Offline data → Durable local queue with resumable upload

  • Each device can retain samples independently through outages.
  • Resumable transfer handles returning connectivity.
  • Queue capacity and fleet-wide upload behavior need sizing.

This keeps buffering close to the cameras without introducing plant gateway storage operations.

Per-camera queues relayed through plant gateway chosen in B1

Offline data → Per-camera durable queues relayed through gateway

  • Camera queues reduce dependence on one plant spool.
  • The gateway can consolidate deferred uploads.
  • Many local queues and gateway relay add operations.

This preserves data during connectivity loss while reducing a gateway spool as a single storage dependency.

Persistent gateway spool chosen in D1

Offline data → Plant gateway persistent spool with deferred upload

  • A single plant buffer can store selected images and upload later.
  • Keeps bulk data transfer separate from inference.
  • Gateway storage and capacity become important dependencies.

This centralizes data relay without making the gateway responsible for model rollout.

ADR3 · How should models reach lines and recover from bad releases? R2Q3C1Q4

Context. Approved models must reach every connected line within 24 hours, and operators must be able to restore a previous model within 15 minutes without the ML team. Rollouts must fit a small operations allocation and a tight infrastructure budget.

Decision. Managed fleet with health-gated canaries (A1); Plant-coordinated cached rollout (B1); Direct artifact polling with approved scheduled waves (C1); Cloud-managed fleet with direct artifact polling (D1).

Managed fleet with health-gated canaries chosen in A1

Fleet control → Managed cloud device fleetModel rollout → Automated canary health-gated rollout with local rollback

  • Central coordination and automated health gates reduce repeated operator work.
  • Local rollback is available at the line.
  • Managed fleet charges and service configuration need cost review.

The added rollout service buys automated staged delivery and checks while keeping recovery local.

Plant-coordinated cached rollout chosen in B1

Fleet control → Plant-local gateway coordinationModel rollout → Gateway-cached staged rollout with local rollback

  • Cached models can be distributed within the plant during cloud outages.
  • Local staged rollback supports operator recovery.
  • Gateway coordination and maintenance increase staff burden.

The gateway favors local autonomy and outage resilience at the cost of additional plant operations.

Direct artifact polling with approved scheduled waves chosen in C1

Fleet control → Device-direct object storage pollingModel rollout → Scheduled automatic rollout waves with operator approval and local A/B model slots

  • Avoids a dedicated fleet-control service.
  • Approval, scheduled waves, and A/B slots combine controlled rollout with accessible reversal.
  • Polling and duplicate model slots consume bandwidth and local storage.

This balances low central service overhead with human approval and local recovery.

Cloud-managed fleet with direct artifact polling chosen in D1

Fleet control → Managed cloud device fleetModel rollout → Device-direct artifact polling with local rollback

  • Cloud coordination tracks devices while connected devices fetch artifacts directly.
  • Local rollback does not require ML staff.
  • Polling intervals and the separate gateway data path need operational ownership.

This hybrid keeps deployment out of the gateway while retaining cloud fleet visibility.
